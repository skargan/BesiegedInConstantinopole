61: </b> Ottomans try to fill the Moat </b> 
62: The moat protecting the Theodosian Walls is a serious issue for the Ottoman war machine and they have to deal with it before they can hope to breach the walls themselves. <br>
63: Turkish engineers are brilliant in their craft and soon devise a plan on how to fill the moat. They bring forth massive wheeled palisades, which are used to shield their soldiers from the arrows of your archers. These structures are wheeled close to the edge of the moat with careful precision. Within the protection of the palisades, Ottoman soldiers toil relentlessly to deliver soil, rocks, and debris into the moat, forming long human chains, passing basket after basket of dirt into the moat. <br>
64: Your troops don’t stand still – the marksmen pick off any labourer or engineer who unwisely pokes his head outside of the protection, while your cannons, although few in number, destroy some of the palisades and kill those who tried to hide behind them. However, bodies of the dead also soon end up as part of the material used to fill the moat. <br>
65: However despite all of this, at the end of the day, the moat is more full than it was in the morning and the Turks are one step closer to getting into your City.

72: </b> Ottomans construct a Siege Tower </b> 
73: The Ottoman forces, relentless in their pursuit of Constantinople's conquest, embark on the construction of a colossal siege tower — a towering behemoth designed to pierce the city's defenses. <br>
74: Rising amidst the Ottoman encampment, the siege tower becomes a looming symbol of their determination. Crafted from thick wooden planks, reinforced with metal, and adorned with protective coverings of animal skins to protect it from fire, this war machine represents a clear threat to the City's walls. <br>
75: Skilled Turkish engineers and artisans toil ceaselessly, assembling the intricate components of the siege tower with meticulous precision. As the tower takes shape, you can see that its sturdy base sits on sixteen massive wheels and supports a platform large enough to carry an infantry contingent. At the top of the tower there is a shield-bridge to protect the soldiers from the arrows while approaching the city and then to act as a bridge and allow the attackers to cross the gap between the siege tower and the battlements. Ladders are attached to its sides, offering a means for Ottoman soldiers to quickly swarm over the city walls once the tower reaches its destination. <br>
76: As the Ottoman siege tower reaches completion, it is clear that it will be used in the next assault and that we should be ready for it. <br>

85: You notice a serious commotion in the centre of the Ottoman camp. Their engineers and artisans are clearly busy with some sinister project, so you order your troops to watch out. Soon, a colossal structure takes form in the besieging camp — a battering ram, a formidable siege instrument designed to smash the gates of Constantine’s City to pieces. <br>
86: Turks have used a giant thousand-year-old oak for its basis and then reinforced it with layers of metal and planks. Skilled craftsmen labor tirelessly, fashioning the ram's head into a menacing shape, a prelude to the impending assault on the city's formidable gates. <br>
87: The battering ram is housed within a palisade structure which will protect the soldiers pushing it forward and the large wheels will provide mobility, allowing it to be maneuvered to the Gates of St Romanus. <br>
88: Some ask why Ottomans don’t simply rely on their cannons to break down the gates, it is clear that they cannot ensure necessary accuracy over such distances, as our brave archers and counter-cannons will not allow the enemy to approach close enough and give them enough time to break down our gates using gunpowder. In contrast to the artillery, the battering ram is a focused weapon, designed for a singular purpose — to deliver blunt force upon the city gates, powered by muscles and determination of the soldiers who will bring it forth. <br>
89: The air is charged with an aura of impending conflict as the battering ram nears completion. Ottoman soldiers are clearly eager to make use of it in the coming assault and we must be ready for it.

152: The name of these troops literally means “crazy heads”, they are not part of a standing army and usually tend to be bandits, caravan guards, mercenaries or anything in between during the times of peace. However, when a Sultan announces a war, they come in thousands to support him and in hopes of getting rich. They have a reputation for being undisciplined and brutal, notorious for looting and preying on civilians as a result of a lack of regulation and of the expectation that they would support themselves off the land.
153: The rhythmic tramp of boots echoes through the Ottoman camp as Bashi-Bazouks, seasoned in various wars, start organising themselves for the impending assault.
