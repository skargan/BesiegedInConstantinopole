BESIEGED — MONKS AND WOUNDED RECOVERY
Implementation: 1 November 2026. First playable medical service.

DESIGN INTENT
Surviving a battle should leave a human and military burden. Monks preserve part of the army, but those men cannot defend tomorrow's assault. The player must hold ground, reserve provisions and build enough medical capacity. Treatment runs automatically: the meaningful decisions are preparation and resources, not repeated healing clicks.

RULES
Item                       Implemented rule
Workforce                  Existing iHealers, displayed as Monks. No second workforce. Starting data remains 100.
Admission                  After a held-position battle, up to half the actual casualties can enter care, limited by free places.
Abandoned positions        No new patients rescued. Existing patients remain in the infirmary.
Capacity                   One occupied place per monk. Two battles cannot reuse the same occupied places.
Unit identity              Infantry, archers, cavalry and Varangian Guard recover into their original units.
Recovery                   Fifteen completed treatment days. Patients are absent from active combat strength throughout.
Provisions                 One extra provision per five patients treated each day, rounded up once across all units.
Shortages                  Normal civilian rations are taken first. Remaining supplies and monks limit treatment; excess patients wait.
Triage                     Treat patients closest to recovery first. Equal-stage places follow the existing patient mix.
Interrupted treatment      Waiting days do not count. No unannounced random deaths. Losing monks does not delete existing patients.
More monks                 Five for 1,000 gold; arrive after five days through the existing recruitment queue. No major action.
Manpower                   Wounded regular soldiers remain committed and cannot be recruited again. Recovery adds no population.
Reporting                  Battle outcome: admitted and permanent losses. Daily report: treatment cost, returns and patients remaining.
Interface                  Recruitment and recovery > Infirmary; also Specialists and City at a Glance > Infirmary.
Persistence                Existing campaign integer store; old saves start with an empty infirmary. Normal saves retain progress.

EXAMPLE
With 100 monks and an empty infirmary, 200 casualties from a held position produce 100 wounded and 100 permanent losses. The wounded need 20 extra provisions per full treatment day and return after fifteen such days. Until then, the infirmary is full. Building a medical service after a battle cannot retrospectively recover men already lost.

SOURCE RECONCILIATION AND BOUNDARIES
Core mechanics 1.3 proposed instant casualty reduction of Healers x 1d5 when a wall was held. That routine was not implemented. This version explicitly replaces the proposal with delayed recovery, following the later War Beyond the Walls medical direction. Rescue on withdrawal remains unavailable. The 50% rescue ceiling, one place per monk and provision ratio are initial tuning values, not historical claims.

The implemented intake routes are phased assaults (including their sorties and incidents), legacy assaults, and sea-wall fighting. Generic troop reductions are deliberately not intake: desertion, executions, missing men, explicit deaths and personnel transfers must not create patients. Naval losses have no crew roster and do not create land soldiers. Individual narrative combat branches need explicit survivor wording before being adapted. Emperor health and character injury timers remain separate systems.

Losses so far remains a cumulative count of troops removed from service; it includes wounded and is labelled accordingly. The infirmary separately records those returned. The existing manpower system still permits replacement of permanent losses; this feature does not redesign population recruitment.

Medical allocation adds no random rolls and does not change battle lethality. It improves campaign endurance after successful defences. Balance review should compare monks hired, peak occupancy, treatment provision use and returning soldiers against assault frequency and permanent losses. No medicine technology or additional resource is added in this batch.

POTENTIAL OTHER ROLES — SUGGESTIONS ONLY, NOT IMPLEMENTED
1. Sanitation and burial. A temporary assignment reduces disease/outbreak risk after overcrowding or heavy losses. It occupies monks who would otherwise nurse soldiers; burial does not become a second automatic morale income.
2. Refuge and poor relief. Open a monastery to refugees or distribute food during famine. Tie this to existing provisions, public support and church politics. The difficult choice is whom limited food and space can protect.
3. Mediation across the Church Union divide. A named monk can broker a local truce between rival clergy, improving cooperation at a political cost. This should be a character/event decision, not an unlimited 'gain faith' button.
4. Preservation of the City's memory. Evacuate manuscripts, relics and patient registers if defeat approaches. This competes for transport and time; the epilogue remembers what survived even if the city falls. It offers a meaningful secondary aim without changing the main victory condition.

RECOMMENDATION
Develop sanitation/burial next: it shares the medical workforce and connects battle losses, overcrowding, food and unrest. Keep theological mediation and preservation as finite authored stories. Do not let monks become universal producers of health, morale, secrets and money.
