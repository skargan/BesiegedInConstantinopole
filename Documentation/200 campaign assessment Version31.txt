PRIORITISED DECISION LIST

1. P0 - AI tactic tables silently stop operations
Status:Runtime pattern plus scene-source confirmation
Evidence:140/200 campaigns had no phased assault;46 of these lasted at least100 siege days. Three tactic tables are empty: TestWall,AttackWall,SendAssault. Advance contains a null action.
A. Populate and validate the existing tables; fail the build for empty/null actions. RECOMMENDED.
B. Replace tables with a small explicit, data-driven tactic catalogue.
C. Add a fallback bombardment/assault while gradually migrating the tables.
Implementation note:Repair legal actions first; choose their weights after a new sweep.

2. P1 - Victory can occur without defending an assault
Status:Observed
Evidence:All3 victories reached240 days with0 phased assaults (runs16,195,200).
A. Keep240-day victory, but restore credible Ottoman pressure first. RECOMMENDED.
B. Require a final major assault or relief event before victory.
C. Offer negotiated or supply-collapse victory paths with visible prerequisites.
Implementation note:Do not infer a1.5percent human win rate from these automated policies.

3. P1 - Casualty asymmetry remains extreme
Status:Observed
Evidence:Four runs exceeded100 attackers killed per defender in at least one assault:45/310044=5000:9;121/310120=5000:39;133/310132=5000:48;134/310133=5000:25.
A. Cap exposure/lethality per phase and preserve a larger retreating attacker fraction. RECOMMENDED for prototype.
B. Retain lethal defence but make repulse primarily morale and retreat, separating routed from killed.
C. Raise defender attrition/counterfire while retaining current attacker lethality.
Implementation note:Retest identical assault states and seeds; do not multiply all casualties globally.

4. P1 - Political approval dominates campaign endings
Status:Observed design signal
Evidence:175/200 ended through support or morale collapse:96 noble,49 army,28 public,2 clergy. No city-wall-loss ending occurred.
A. Use warning thresholds and a recoverable political crisis before defeat. RECOMMENDED.
B. Keep instant defeat atzero but expose forecasts and stronger recovery tools.
C. Differentiate consequences: noble obstruction, clergy unrest, desertion, then eventual collapse.
Implementation note:Random event choices are not skilled political management; counts diagnose the current failure mix, not optimal-play difficulty.

5. P1 - Food-shortage events repeat excessively
Status:Observed
Evidence:166/200 runs encountered foodShortage;4112 distinct run/turn encounters;one run encountered it63 times.
A. Persistent rationing policy with a daily consequence and occasional new escalation. RECOMMENDED.
B. Keep daily shortage choice, add opt-in repeat-last-policy and compact reporting.
C. Multi-stage starvation chain with escalating, distinct decisions and a clear terminal condition.
Implementation note:Preserve food pressure; reduce repeated identical interaction.

6. P1 - Fleet rewards arrive unpredictably and too far apart
Status:Observed plus source confirmation
Evidence:276 fleet-report encounters across152 runs. Repeat intervals ranged30-105 turns,median45. Reports share a random narrative pool and cooldown.
A. Fixed fortnightly reports outside the narrative lottery. RECOMMENDED; other agent has an unintegrated candidate.
B. Accumulate mission progress daily and resolve at a visible threshold.
C. Retain random reports but guarantee a maximum wait.
Implementation note:Rebalance report rewards after changing frequency; unchanged rewards every14days increase economic output.
