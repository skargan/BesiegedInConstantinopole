<b>Siege Preparation — Our Turks</b>: <br>Orhan Çelebi, a prince of the Ottoman house and a potential rival to Mehmed’s throne, has long lived in Constantinople under imperial protection and watch. His presence has been useful in negotiations with the sultans; now it makes him a marked man. He requests an audience, offering the service of his Turkish followers in the city’s defence.<br>Other Turkish residents await your decision alongside them: soldiers in imperial service, household retainers, merchants and their families. Some have lived here for years. With preparations for the siege underway, petitions have reached the palace demanding their expulsion.<br>Orhan addresses you.<br>“My lord, you know what awaits me if Mehmed enters this city. My men know it too. Give us a place on the walls, and we will defend it. But their families must have your protection. They cannot stand watch while wondering whether their own houses will be searched or their children driven out.”<br>Your councillors disagree over how far that protection should extend. Orhan’s followers have good reason to fight, but he cannot answer for every Turkish household. One councillor asks that correspondence and departures be watched: a letter carried to relatives across the straits could also report which gates are poorly guarded. Another reminds you that several of the men now under suspicion have served the Empire faithfully for years. They await your ruling.
