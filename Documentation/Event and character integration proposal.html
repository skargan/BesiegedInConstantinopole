Besieged event and character integration proposal

Review of Ev.docx, 21 October 2026. This proposal consolidates repeated notes and connects the historical siege episodes to the current preparation, fleet, intelligence, character and political systems. The new chains and casting rules below are proposals, not implemented features. The separately authorised five character preparations and one-invitation arrival mechanism form the first foundation.

Recommendation: build a recognisable siege around a variable supporting cast. Historical developments supply pressure and opportunities; the player's previous decisions determine what can actually happen. Limit simultaneous personal stories so each visitor becomes memorable.

A changing court with a coherent campaign

Keep the Emperor and Ottoman command as the dependable centre. Established local figures such as Notaras and Sphrantzes have reliable routes into play; their availability still follows choices, departures and deaths. Select visiting specialists and personal storylines when the campaign starts, then save that selection. Reopening a menu or loading a checkpoint must not redraw the cast.

For the expanded Call on Adventurers, offer three dossiers drawn from different roles: one military or maritime visitor, one engineer, and one scholar or diplomat. Let the player invite one. The current first version offers six candidates and one invitation; the three-dossier draw is a proposed refinement. Additional visitors may emerge through earned opportunities, with a suggested cap of three optional recruited visitors per campaign and two active personal chains at once. Finish, suspend or abandon one chain before introducing another major personal story.

Randomness should decide who is reachable and which complications occur. Once an invitation is accepted, show its expected arrival. Delays should be explained and bounded; do not turn every promised guest into another recruitment lottery. An absent specialist should change the solution to a problem rather than make ordinary survival impossible. Grant makes countermining more effective; engineers can still attempt it without him.

Use a separate content seed for casting, dialogue order and hidden identities. Combat rolls retain their own state. That prevents browsing the court from changing tomorrow's casualties. Character dossiers record location, availability, loyalties, obligations and promises made by the Emperor. Political allegiance, personal loyalty and practical expertise remain separate.

The harbour war

The Relief Fleet

Historical anchor: 20 April 1453; the naval relief engagement is described in Barbaro's diary. Use the date as the first eligible window, with the historical convoy distinct from other purchased foreign aid.

People and prerequisites: Diedo and the harbour captains, with Barbaro as an observer if present. A relief convoy must still be afloat. If the Ottoman blockade has already been broken, use an arrival and allocation scene instead of manufacturing a battle.

Sequence: sails sighted → decide whether to commit an escort, cover them from shore, or preserve the defensive fleet → resolve the approach → unload supplies and receive survivors. Escorts expose ships but improve rescue prospects; shore support consumes ammunition. The player may save part of the cargo even after a military setback.

Consequences: record the actual surviving vessels and cargo once. Success improves morale and can provoke Mehmed; losses create rescue, prisoner or missing-cargo followups. An extraordinary success captures an Ottoman officer or standard, requiring strong preparations and a costly commitment. It does not automatically end the blockade.

Ships over the Land

Historical anchor: the Ottoman transfer into the Golden Horn in April 1453. Intelligence about its preparation should be uncertain; the resulting naval access is a concrete strategic change.

Sequence: unusual timber requisitions or reports of road works → investigate or dismiss → sabotage rollers, supplies or work crews; pressure Galata; reposition ships; or prepare the sea garrison → resolve the transfer. Better intelligence gives earlier notice and more targeted choices. Galata's goodwill affects access and witnesses, while its political status affects what the Emperor can demand.

Consequences: partial disruption delays the move or reduces ships arriving. Successful transfer raises sea-wall pressure and forces the existing allocation decision between main-wall and sea-wall garrisons. The Great Chain remains intact: it has been bypassed. If no viable Ottoman fleet survives, this operation cannot occur. A complete prevention is a rare combined achievement requiring intelligence, access and resources, rather than a single lucky button.

The Night Attack

Historical anchor: the failed 28 April attack associated with Giacomo Coco. Treat accusations of betrayal as testimony and factional interpretation, not settled proof against every Genoese character.

Sequence: Coco proposes burning the ships → select commander and secrecy arrangements → launch quickly, wait for broader participation, or cancel → battle and rescue → accounting for the missing. Waiting can improve equipment and coordination while increasing exposure; it should not be universally foolish.

Consequences: surviving Ottoman ships determine the continuing sea threat. Greek Fire changes the available method but still requires delivery. Friendly casualties damage relationships with the captains involved. A leak investigation may uncover an actual contact, a careless clerk or a convenient scapegoat. If the overland transfer was prevented, replace this with an optional outer-anchorage raid rather than spawning ships inside the Horn.

The Venetian Galleys Dispute

Basis: use the supplied May equipment dispute as an episode requiring entry-level source checking before a fixed date or exact historical demand is asserted.

Trigger: defence supplies are scarce while usable Venetian shipping remains. Diedo argues for naval readiness; wall commanders want matériel; merchants and families see the ships as their last passage out.

Choices: strip selected vessels, requisition a limited amount with compensation, negotiate a temporary loan, or preserve the ships. Show the precise effect on readiness, available materials and evacuation capacity. The earlier Venetian trust settlement alters negotiation costs. Taking equipment by force may provoke a refusal or departure; protecting escape capacity may anger the garrison. Both preserving the fleet and stripping it can be defensible in different campaigns.

The underground war and the meaning of omens

The Underground War

Basis: documented mining and countermining during the siege. Grant becomes the strongest recurring voice if hired; a named ordinary foreman provides the fallback.

Sequence: a listening post detects work → survey the suspected gallery → choose countershaft, flooding, smoke or a controlled collapse → deal with trapped workers or a captured sapper → confront a second, better concealed attempt. Completed wall repairs unlock prepared galleries as the supplied notes suggest. A single repair should unlock the method, not permanently remove the threat.

Costs and branches: materials, engineers and time compete with visible wall repairs. Prisoners may offer a map that requires verification. Reckless excavation can damage the defenders' own foundations. Discovery permits a recoverable emergency; catastrophic breaches require accumulated neglect or a knowingly risky choice. An extraordinary branch collapses a major Ottoman gallery and exposes a supply route, opening a later operation rather than granting a permanent defence multiplier.

The Lunar Eclipse

Historical anchor: 22 May 1453. The astronomical event can occur regardless of strategy; its interpretation must respond to the City's condition.

Voices: Scholarios, Isidore, Plethon or another scholar if available, alongside witnesses from the public. Offer a vigil, a measured explanation, a public appearance by the Emperor, or a faction-backed interpretation. An astronomical explanation should not make medieval characters instantly abandon religious meaning.

Consequences: adjust morale, cooperation and the political standing of the chosen speakers. An earlier prediction makes reassurance more credible. Coercive suppression may stop a gathering while driving rumours underground. Extraordinary interpretation can motivate exceptional volunteers or reconciliation, but does not prove supernatural causation or repair walls by itself. If the campaign has already ended, this becomes an epilogue reference rather than a resurrected turn.

Saving the Legacy of Rome

Make this an optional chain with its own remembered outcomes, starting when Anna Notaras, Sphrantzes or a scholar identifies what may be lost. Anna's presence and specific activities require a creative-liberty note; do not imply that every proposed rescue episode is documented.

Sequence: inventory endangered collections and people → secure packing, escorts and a destination → choose what a limited ship or convoy can carry → face a departure crisis → trace what survives. Competing loads include manuscripts and archives, relics, tools and craftspeople, and families. Avoid a single score that declares books inherently more valuable than people.

Ships committed to evacuation cannot simultaneously deliver food or defend the harbour. Foreign contacts affect safe destinations. Saving workshops may help an enduring City or an exile community; saving fiscal records may preserve claims and obligations; rescued texts may enable a later intellectual revival. Military defeat still permits a meaningful legacy ending, but does not erase the fate of people left behind. If the City survives, collections can remain or return without inventing an exile merely to complete the chain.

Palingenesia and the Guard

Use Palingenesia — The Rebirth of Rome as the consolidated name; earlier Renaissance labels in the notes become working titles. Plethon's claimed feigned death is fictional. His death date is disputed in scholarship, so Methodology should explain the disagreement rather than assert an uncontested 1452 death.

Build six commitments: arrival with the Hellenic retinue; an audience on reform; land and service obligations; a trial fiscal or agricultural reform; a confrontation over religious authority; and the Guard's new oath. Let the player accept practical reforms without automatically adopting Plethon's religion. A final radical settlement should require repeated, explicit choices.

The original fifty Hellenic warriors can join the existing elite formation. Preserve its breach role and present-day weapons. Praetorian Guard is an institutional outcome, not a free second army. Kjold remains captain at first. He may support reform, demand guarantees for his men, resign honourably or oppose it according to decisions. Sir Raoul de Vaucelles is an optional fictional rival, not an obligatory replacement.

Cyriacus is better used as curator, ideological advocate and author of ceremonies than as combat commander. Giving an antiquarian command of an elite formation needs a convincing invented apprenticeship and displaces more suitable soldiers. Scholarios should oppose particular commitments, organise resistance and sometimes negotiate; he should not mechanically sabotage every technological improvement.

The Legacy of Varna

Hans Mägest offers the most personal route: an escaped captive brings remembered names and hard-earned knowledge, some of it outdated. Verification, a missing companion and a dispute over revenge make a three-to-five-event chain. His early escape is the divergence; surviving Varna does not make him omniscient about Mehmed's current plans.

Differentiate the Burgundians rather than recruiting both by default. Waleran de Wavrin is the organiser of a renewed expedition, concerned with coalitions and commitments. Geoffroy de Thoisy is the more volatile maritime entrepreneur, useful for aggressive ventures but bringing commercial and political complications. Their actual expeditions warrant fuller research before these proposed personalities become definitive characterisations. A rumour that King Władysław survived should remain a rare disputed claim, with costs of investigation and opportunities for imposture; no automatic royal reveal.

The Crimean alternative

Hacı Giray should be an independent diplomatic actor with his own conflict with Genoese interests. Independence from Ottoman suzerainty does not imply hostility to Mehmed: the sources describe cooperation against Caffa in 1454. The player must offer something that changes his calculation.

Use Giosafat or a Genoese intermediary to obtain an audience, negotiate grain and safe passage, then face demands concerning trade rights or rivals. Tatar mercenary recruitment is a separate contract. Access requires a viable route, shipping and the appropriate fleet mission; a diplomatic agreement must not teleport provisions through a closed strait. A deep alliance can strain Genoese relations and therefore affect Galata, captains and other relief routes.

Implementation priorities and safeguards against repetition

First connect the harbour chain and countermining to existing fleet, chain, Galata, engineering and garrison state. Then implement legacy preservation and the eclipse. Develop Palingenesia as the first large character programme only after the smaller chains can schedule, suspend and conclude reliably. Add the remaining visitors in small groups with distinct stories.

Adopt the four prerequisite ideas in Ev.docx: completed wall repairs enable countermining galleries; retained Orban enables specialist artillery crews; open straits enable a Black Sea grain agreement with deliveries tied to fleet orders; recovered Greek Fire enables fleet equipment after a separate investment. Each is a meaningful earned option, with an ordinary fallback.

Every node needs prerequisites, a trigger window, a spent flag, affected characters, visible costs, hidden state, followups, expiry handling and a fallback if its essential person or resource disappears. Do not pay twice for the same rescue, cargo or recruitment. Prevent two chains from using the same ship or officer simultaneously. Show an expected consequence when the Emperor can know it; keep secret identities and infiltration effects outside player-facing result summaries.

Characters who are absent can send letters or be replaced by role-appropriate minor figures. Their distinctive personal branches should remain absent, preserving replay value. Historical dates become opportunities or pressures, except physical events such as the eclipse. Previous victories must change later scenes. Otherwise the game rewards the player and then quietly ignores the reward.

Research consulted and work still required

Ev.docx is the design brief, not a historical authority. This pass examined the following online material or available excerpts. Bibliographic citations inside the brief without a recoverable source are research leads, not consulted works.

  • Niccolò Barbaro, siege diary, translated extract hosted by De Re Militari. Eyewitness perspective, especially useful for naval chronology and the eclipse; partisan accusations and troop counts require corroboration.
  • Treccani, Niccolò Barbaro. Biographical context and diary references.
  • John Monfasani, Pletho's Date of Death and the Burning of His Laws, Byzantinische Zeitschrift 98(2), 459–463, DOI 10.1515/BYZS.2005.459. Abstract/excerpt consulted; full article not examined.
  • Encyclopaedia Iranica, Giosafat Barbaro; Treccani, Giovanni Fontana. Distinguish the inventor from later namesakes.
  • TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, Hacı Giray I. Especially important for his independent interests and the 1454 Caffa episode.
  • Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie, Hans Mägest. Historical reference entry; early escape remains invented.
  • Arjo Vanderjagt, De Bourgondische vloot in de Oriënt, Madoc, 1999. Expedition context; revisit primary accounts before assigning exact later whereabouts.
  • Scholarly introduction on Cyriacus, hosted by NYU ISAW. Relevant excerpt discusses the death-date evidence; entire volume not read.
